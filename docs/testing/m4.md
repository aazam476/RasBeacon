# Milestone 4: hardware test plan (WireGuard VPN and kill switch)

**Goal:** confirm devices' traffic goes through your WireGuard VPN, that
nothing leaks when the VPN breaks with the kill switch on, that the kill
switch off falls back and recovers, and that the clock survives power cuts.

The CI tests already check the kill switch against a real WireGuard server
in simulated networks. This plan checks it on the Pi with your server.

## What you need

- Your Pi 4 + Archer T3U with the latest image, set up, and connected to
  the internet (home Ethernet or Wi-Fi uplink).
- A WireGuard server and a `.conf` file for RasBeacon. See
  [docs/wireguard-server.md](../wireguard-server.md).
- A phone on RasBeacon's hotspot, and a laptop if you can (for step 5).

Copy the **report template** at the end and fill it in.

## 1. Import and connect

1. Open the **VPN** page, choose your `.conf` file (or paste it), tap
   **Import**.
2. Tick **Use the VPN** (leave **Kill switch** ticked) and **Save**.
3. Within about 10 seconds, the **Status** page should say *Connected:
   your devices' traffic goes through the VPN* with a recent handshake.
4. On the phone, open a "what is my IP" site (for example
   `https://ifconfig.me`).
5. **Report:** connected yes/no; the IP shown on the phone (is it your
   VPN server's?); any error from the import.

## 2. DNS goes through the VPN

1. On the phone, open [dnsleaktest.com](https://www.dnsleaktest.com) and
   run the standard test.
2. **Report:** the DNS servers listed. They should belong to your VPN's DNS
   (the `DNS =` line), not your home internet provider.

## 3. Kill switch ON: nothing leaks

1. Stop the VPN server (on your server: `sudo systemctl stop wg-quick@wg0`,
   or switch off the VPN on your provider's side; or, simplest, change the
   server's port in *Server details* to a wrong one and save).
2. Wait 3 to 4 minutes (the tunnel is judged broken when the last
   handshake is over 3 minutes old). The Status page should say *Not
   connected. The kill switch is on...*
3. On the phone, try to open a few websites. **They must not load.**
4. **Report:** did any site load (that would be a leak)? What did the
   Status page say?
5. Start the server again (or fix the port). **Report:** how long until
   websites work again?

## 4. Kill switch OFF: falls back and returns

1. On the VPN page, untick **Kill switch**, **Save**.
2. Stop the server again, wait about 4 minutes.
3. **Report:** does the Status page say *using the internet WITHOUT the
   VPN*, and do websites load (with your home IP on ifconfig.me)?
4. Start the server. After about a minute the Status page should be back
   to *Connected*. **Report:** how long it took, and the IP on ifconfig.me.
5. Tick **Kill switch** again and **Save**.

## 5. Boot with the VPN on (no leak at startup)

1. With the VPN on and the kill switch on, stop the VPN server.
2. Pull the Pi's power, wait 5 seconds, plug it back in.
3. As soon as the hotspot reappears, join it with the phone and try a
   website repeatedly for 2 minutes.
4. **Report:** did anything load? (It must not.) Then start the server and
   check the phone gets online.

## 6. Speed through the VPN (optional)

With the laptop on the hotspot and the VPN connected, run a speed test (for
example [fast.com](https://fast.com)) with the VPN on and then off.
**Report:** both results. (The Pi 4 does WireGuard at a few hundred Mbit/s;
the hotspot or your internet is usually the limit.)

## 7. Clock

1. Turn the VPN on and confirm it connects.
2. Unplug the Pi for at least 10 minutes, then plug it in at a place where
   it gets internet as before.
3. **Report:** does the VPN reconnect by itself within a couple of minutes?
   (If the clock started wrong, the handshake would fail; RasBeacon moves
   the clock forward to the last saved time at boot.)
